# Phase 3 — Community Trust-Building (Research)

Researched 2026-09-22. Goal per the plan: answer real technical questions with real expertise and
a profile link, never a pitch — this reaches buyers before they ever search commercially.

## Shortlist (5 communities)

1. **r/architecture** — general architecture community (students, professionals, enthusiasts).
   Broadest reach of the five; good for design-process/BIM-workflow answers, not just software
   troubleshooting.
2. **r/Revit** (~7k members) — Revit-specific Q&A. High-signal audience: people actively doing
   BIM production work, closest match to this business's actual service.
3. **r/AutoCAD** (~10k members) — AutoCAD-specific Q&A, largest of the CAD-tool subreddits.
   Matches the CAD/permit-drafting side of the business.
4. **r/BIM** — general BIM discussion, less tool-specific than r/Revit, good for LOD/workflow/
   coordination questions (ties directly into the LOD guide content already live).
5. **r/rhino** — Rhino/Grasshopper community, matches the parametric/computational design work
   already shown in the portfolio (facade scripting, diagrid patterns) — smaller than the others
   but the best-fit audience for that specific specialty.

Autodesk's own community forums (forums.autodesk.com) are a reasonable 6th option if the five
above turn out too slow-moving, but weren't prioritized here since Reddit has faster
question-turnover and existing SEO/backlink value from post links.

## Cadence and rules (per the plan — do NOT deviate from this)

- **Answer real questions only.** Never post unprompted. Never link the site unless it's directly
  relevant to the specific question asked, and even then, sparingly.
- A profile link in the Reddit account bio/flair is enough exposure on its own — most answers
  shouldn't need an inline link at all.
- Light, honest cadence — a few genuinely useful answers a week is plenty. This is a slow-compound
  channel, not a growth hack; over-posting reads as spam and can get a new account
  shadowbanned/removed, which would undo the whole point.
- New Reddit accounts have very limited posting ability initially (karma/age gates on some
  subreddits) — if the account posting isn't already established with some history, budget a
  couple weeks of just answering low-stakes questions before this becomes a meaningful channel.

## Status

Research only — no accounts created, no posts made. This is inherently a slow, manual,
ongoing activity (not a one-time setup task), so there's no further "finishing" step here beyond
picking a Reddit account to use and starting to answer questions when time allows.
